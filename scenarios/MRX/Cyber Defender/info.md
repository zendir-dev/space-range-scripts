# Cyber Defender

## Introduction

**Cyber Defender** is a one-hour space-cyber workshop in which duty crews operate a medium Earth orbit imagery and signals-intelligence spacecraft over a busy maritime corridor. The spacecraft begins healthy. Over the session, environmental effects and a co-orbital rogue introduce a catalog of cyber and subsystem threats: GPS spoofing and jamming, solar-array degradation, telemetry injection, storage and attitude faults, command replay, and uplink and downlink interference. Teams must detect each effect, tell a shared environmental problem apart from an attack on their own radio link, apply the right mitigation from the console, and file a forensic after-action report.

## Focus Theme

The scenario focuses on threat recognition, attribution, and recovery under overlapping symptoms. Operators learn to match telemetry signatures to a specific effect, choose frequency hopping, key rotation, component reset, or wait-it-out as appropriate, and use electromagnetic sensing to tie hostile activity to a hidden co-orbital actor. Maritime imagery remains in the picture, but the teaching objective is keeping a contested spacecraft serviceable when several faults and attacks arrive at once.

### Who it is for

- Satellite operators and duty crews who need to keep a spacecraft healthy while GPS, power, storage, attitude, and the RF link are under attack.
- Cyber, RF, and communications specialists who need to recognize spoofing, jamming, replay, and data injection from live telemetry.
- Defense and allied training audiences who need a practical space-cyber exercise with forensic reporting, not a pure ISR collection drill.

## Learning Objectives

- Recognize the telemetry signatures of GPS spoofing, GPS jamming, uplink and downlink jamming, telemetry injection, replay, and subsystem faults across power, storage, and attitude control.
- Distinguish environmental effects that every crew sees from targeted effects aimed at a single communications link.
- Choose the right mitigation for each effect, including frequency hopping, key rotation, and component reset, then confirm from telemetry that the response worked.
- Cross-check a suspect GPS fix against independent references such as magnetometer data, camera geography, and the expected ground track.
- Use the EM sensor and spectrum data to characterize a hidden co-orbital adversary and its transmissions.
- Inspect telemetry packets for injected content that does not belong in the data.
- Keep a timeline of observations and operator actions, and report findings as a coherent forensic assessment.

## Narrative

Duty crews on the Maritime Cyber Watch program take control of a defender spacecraft providing imagery and SIGINT over the Hormuz and Malacca corridors. The first minutes are familiarization: log in, establish a ground-station pass, take a health baseline, and begin maritime collection. Then the environment turns. GPS solutions drift or drop out over a spoof and jam region. A solar array degrades. Storage fills. A reaction wheel sticks. Injected strings appear in telemetry packets. None of these effects wait politely for the last one to finish.

Meanwhile a co-orbital rogue, callsign PHANTOM, has been listening. It replays captured commands onto the uplink, then jams the team's frequencies and later the downlink over specific ground stations. Crews must stabilize the link or the failed subsystem first, decide whether what they see is environmental, onboard, or adversary-driven, and only then write the forensic answers. Scripted threats complete within the first three-quarters of the hour; the remaining time is for scoring and after-action notes.

## Synopsis Teaser

A maritime watch spacecraft is on station over one of the world's busiest shipping corridors. The first pass looks routine: establish contact, check health, image the strait. Then the picture fractures. Navigation no longer matches the map. Power and attitude telemetry go non-linear. Packets contain text that should not be there. Commands appear in the schedule that no one on the crew sent. Somewhere nearby, a hidden spacecraft is listening, replaying, and jamming.

Cyber Defender asks operators to do what a real duty crew must do when several crises arrive together: name the effect, separate environment from adversary, apply the mitigation that actually fits, and write down the evidence. It is a space-cyber exercise for teams who need more than a slide deck on threats. They need to feel the signatures, choose a response, and still deliver the report.
