# Lunar Logistics

## Introduction

**Lunar Logistics** is a cislunar operations exercise in which each team commands a logistics microsatellite docked to a shared Lunar Gateway hub. While berthed, operators configure fuel and power interconnects, resupply hub propellant, and keep a low starting battery from collapsing during transfer. Mid-session, lunar geometry puts the spacecraft into a Deep Space Network blackout. After contact returns, teams undock with no thrusters and no rendezvous software, then use cameras and laser range finders to characterize relative motion, inspect a co-orbital rocket body, and survey far-side lunar surface features.

## Focus Theme

The scenario focuses on docked logistics, coupled subsystem diagnosis, and proximity awareness under hard constraints. Fuel transfer is not a single switch: pump power, valves, fuses, and interconnect state all have to line up, and a fault on the power path can stop the flow. Communications planning is equally central. Canberra DSN is the only ground station, a blackout is expected between 10 and 20 minutes, and once the vehicle undocks it cannot return to the hub.

### Who it is for

- Satellite operators and flight controllers who need to practice berthing logistics, power and propellant management, and go/no-go decisions around a predicted comms gap.
- Proximity-operations and inspection teams who must undock, range, and image without thrusters or automated rendezvous software.
- Cislunar, Gateway, and allied training audiences who need a practical lunar-orbit exercise combining resupply, blackout planning, and debris or surface observation.

## Learning Objectives

- Configure fuel and power paths through docking interconnects and execute a hub propellant resupply while monitoring tank levels and battery state.
- Trace transfer interruptions through switched, fused pump power paths and restore or work around the fault from telemetry.
- Plan commanding around a predicted DSN blackout, suspend operations before loss of signal, and run diagnostics when contact returns.
- Command undock under manual constraints (no RPO software, no thrusters) and accept that re-docking is not available.
- Use body-fixed laser range finders to measure range, range rate, and sensor disagreement after separation.
- Characterize a co-orbital rocket body using optical imagery and ranging (range, spin, identity markings).
- Survey the lunar far side with the main camera and correlate imagery against a reference map.

## Narrative

Each team begins already docked to its assigned Gateway port, with a full fuel tank, a battery at only 15% charge, and the interconnect valve closed. Early work is logistics: open the right switches, transfer propellant into the hub, share power without flattening the battery, and watch for a pump-path fault that can halt the flow. The Communications Specialist tracks Canberra DSN. Between 10 and 20 minutes the Moon occults the link. Teams that kept transferring through the gap, or left systems in a bad state, pay for it when contact returns.

After the blackout, remaining docked tasks must finish before anyone undocks. Separation is a one-way impulse. Three laser range finders already sighted on the hub become the relative-motion picture. The docking camera still faces the hub; the main camera points the other way, toward debris and the lunar surface. Operators measure the nearby rocket body, look for markings, and pick up far-side features against the reference map before the hour ends.

## Synopsis Teaser

You are already at the Lunar Gateway, tank full, battery low, and the only Earth link is a single Deep Space Network station. The job looks like logistics: open the valves, move propellant, keep the lights on. Then the Moon takes the link away. When the signal comes back, the hub still needs fuel, the interconnects still have to work, and undocking is a one-way door. There are no thrusters and no automated rendezvous. After you leave, you drift, range, and look.

Lunar Logistics is the cislunar exercise for teams who will one day berth, resupply, and inspect in a place where Earth cannot always talk back. It trains the unglamorous skills that keep a gateway alive: power and fuel routing, blackout discipline, and proximity work with the sensors you actually have, not the ones you wish were installed.
