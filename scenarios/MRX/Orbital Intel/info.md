# Orbital Intel

## Introduction

**Orbital Intel** is a maritime surveillance and space-operations exercise in which teams operate a shared reconnaissance spacecraft across three tasking regions: the Red Sea, the South China Sea, and the coast of Venezuela. Operators collect and interpret orbital imagery, compare vessel behavior against tasking intelligence, identify anomalous AIS patterns, and respond when a hidden co-orbital rogue spacecraft disrupts downlink operations. The session starts as a classical ISR mission and then adds spacecraft-health and contested-link pressure, so teams must move from observation to evidence-based judgment, then into mitigation when the space segment itself becomes contested.

## Focus Theme

The scenario focuses on building a defensible operational picture from imperfect evidence: what is visible, what is reported, what does not match, and what action restores mission effectiveness. Teams practice multi-source reasoning under time pressure, using imagery, AIS, telemetry, electromagnetic awareness, and ground-station link performance together, rather than treating any one source as ground truth.

### Who it is for

- Satellite operators and mission controllers who must support maritime ISR tasking while keeping spacecraft health and communications links alive.
- Intelligence and surveillance teams who need to interpret vessel patterns, suspicious behavior, and mismatched AIS information.
- Defense and allied training audiences who need a practical scenario that combines maritime security, space sensing, and contested communications.

## Learning Objectives

- Use orbital imagery and ground-object context to identify commercial, suspicious, damaged, and construction-related vessels.
- Detect new island construction activity by comparing current observations against a reference maritime brief.
- Cross-check visible vessel position, heading, color, and behavior against provided AIS data to identify false or missing broadcasts.
- Diagnose a spacecraft component fault from telemetry and select the appropriate recovery action.
- Recognize downlink jamming through SNR degradation and ground-station link symptoms.
- Identify a hidden rogue spacecraft using imagery, EM sensor evidence, and frequency analysis.
- Apply frequency hopping as the correct mitigation for rogue downlink interference.
- Summarize findings as a coherent operational assessment across maritime tasking and space-system anomalies.

## Narrative

A reconnaissance spacecraft is assigned a fast-moving maritime surveillance mission across three contested regions. First, operators count vessels and isolate piracy indicators in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. Next they confirm suspected island construction in the Paracel Islands by narrowing the camera field of view and matching what they see against the mission brief. Then they check vessel traffic off Venezuela, where some ships lie about position or heading and one broadcasts no AIS at all.

As the collection campaign unfolds, the spacecraft itself becomes part of the problem. Telemetry shows a battery fault that must be diagnosed and reset. Downlink quality then collapses as a hidden co-orbital object begins jamming the link. Teams must decide which vessels are where they claim to be, which activity has changed the map, and which spacecraft is interfering with the mission, then hop frequency to restore service and report what they found.

## Synopsis Teaser

A reconnaissance spacecraft has been assigned a maritime surveillance mission across three contested regions: piracy indicators in the Red Sea, suspected island construction in the South China Sea, and deceptive AIS behavior off Venezuela. At first the task is to observe, compare, and report. Then the spacecraft itself becomes part of the problem: telemetry shows a component fault, downlink quality collapses, and a hidden co-orbital object begins to look less like background clutter and more like an adversary.

Orbital Intel challenges teams to build the truth from orbit. Which vessels are where they claim to be? Which activity has changed the map? Which spacecraft is interfering with the mission? By the end, operators have practiced not only maritime intelligence collection, but also the space-operations discipline needed to keep that intelligence flowing when the link is under pressure.
